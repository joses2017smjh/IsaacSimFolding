## Why the numbers looked bad

1. **Simulation and camera validity failed independently.** The misleading
   upstream CPU-device advice reached the simulation config and froze cloth.
   Enabling RTX cameras crashed Isaac 5.1 at launch. Storm avoided RTX, but
   originally rendered a fixed garment across switches. A later repair still
   recreated the same USD layer path while camera/attribute handles retained
   it; swallowed exceptions served stale pixels. The newer BHL
   `scripts/cloth/eval_fold.py` uses distinct layers, releases old handles,
   checks all three RGB streams, and propagates rendering failures. Geometric
   scoring alone did not establish observation validity.

2. **Raster adaptation used failed replays as ordinary expert supervision.**
   Audit of all 91 `results/*cap100*.json` records gives:

   | Class | Successful replay captures | Failed replay captures |
   | --- | ---: | ---: |
   | Top_Short | 11 | 7 |
   | Top_Long | 17 | 7 |
   | Pant_Short | 24 | 1 |
   | Pant_Long | 3 | 21 |

   `scripts/finetune_rasterised.py:56-71` retained success only in metadata
   and trained on every capture. Once replay drifts, the demonstration's
   future action is not necessarily appropriate for the observed cloth.
   The strong short-pants/weak long-pants data quality imbalance is a concrete
   explanation to test; it is not proof that data quality is the only cause.

3. **Old validation split adjacent frames across train and validation.**
   `scripts/finetune_rasterised.py:107-110` randomly permutes frames, so low
   validation loss can reflect nearly identical states from the same episode.
   Its task prompt at line 172 also differs from the rollout's default task.
   The new BHL production training already fixes the split/supervision issue.

4. **Stage 3 was never an executable RECAP training implementation.**
   `scripts/trainer_loop.py:218-229` unconditionally raises
   `NotImplementedError` for the gradient update. Worker output at
   `scripts/rollout_worker.py:226-238` contains episode metadata, not the
   RGB/state/action trajectories needed to train. Do not queue that legacy
   trainer as a functioning reinforcement-learning method.

5. **Candidate selection cannot score the candidate's actions.**
   `scripts/stage_policies.py:175-181` scores only pooled observation-prefix
   features. Lines 250-256 reuse that same observation for every sampled
   action chunk, so the deterministic prefix scores tie. Lines 268-272 also
   execute only the first action for n>1, while n=1 follows the ordinary
   action queue. Candidate-count comparisons confound control frequency with
   sampling cost. A useful candidate selector needs an action-conditioned
   critic or a validated predictive model, plus matched execution horizons.

6. **The value-head calibration result has limited scope.**
   `scripts/train_value.py:139-160` joins replay outcome labels to original
   path-traced demonstration frames, not the replay observations, and
   `:178-180` randomly splits frames. ECE 0.0717 is not an independent
   on-policy Storm calibration result. The old MCE was also 0.4383. Do not
   use that gate alone to justify action ranking or reliable failure alarms.

7. **The precise renderer causal claim is overstated.**
   `results/action_fidelity_015000.json` uses 120 samples from six episodes
   and mean-baseline MSE 0.30100. The Storm shadow artifact uses 364 replay
   steps from episode 25 and denominator 0.19587. This is not the same paired
   sample set, and replay itself can drift. Raster mismatch is supported by
   the overall evidence and adaptation results; an exactly isolated
   0.902/1.014 renderer-only causal drop is not. The newer BHL folding note
   still inherits that stronger wording and should be corrected when next edited.

Other historical problems were resolved: wrist cameras originally pointed at
empty table, ROS/USD camera conventions disagreed, UV-seam duplicate vertices
made some cloth invisible, and a first raster dataset tiled one action across
50 positions. The legacy job ledger records these fixes. Do not repeat their
old diagnoses as current blockers or overwrite historical artifacts.
